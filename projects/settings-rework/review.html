Open spec questions — these change the build

Answer these before anyone implements. Two of them make parts of the prototypes wrong rather than incomplete.

SPEC-1The add-on rate card doesn't exist anywhere in the UIHigh

All three prototypes show a live price as you change quantities. To do that I had to invent per-block rates for transactions, seats, smart rules and invoicing.

Evidence: the live steppers on Manage subscription and Choose your plan commit via "Update plan" with no price displayed at any point. The only rates visible anywhere are the two bundled tiers — "Up to 20 invoices – $49.00" and "Up to 3 rules – $19.99" — and neither maps to a per-unit rate.

Send me the real rate card and I'll swap the numbers in all three in one pass. Until then the totals demonstrate the pattern, not the amounts — don't user-test pricing comprehension on these figures.
SPEC-2"All users see all functions" contradicts the live invite modalHigh

You told me every user sees everything, so I built one view. But production's own invite dialog defines the Member role as the opposite.

Evidence, verbatim from the Add user modal on Organization settings: Manager — "Full access including billing, user management and platform connection." Member — "Limited rights without access to billing, user management and platform connection."

Either that copy is stale and should be rewritten, or Members need a distinct view in all three prototypes (billing cards read-only or absent). This is a one-line answer with a real build consequence.
SPEC-3Pro Standard ↔ Max is undocumented in the UIMedium

The toggle exists on the Pro card and changes the price, but nothing on the page says what Max actually buys.

Evidence: toggling Max on /organizations/billing?action=UPGRADE updates the price; no feature row, tooltip or accordion entry mentions Max. I inferred it raises included quantities and support tier.

Confirm what Max changes. If it's a support tier rather than volume, my comparison rows are wrong and the toggle belongs next to the support rows, not the price.
SPEC-4Production isn't responsive; the prototypes areMedium

I added breakpoints because the layouts needed them, which is new behaviour rather than a match.

Evidence: rendering both pages at 1280, 1024, 768 and 390px produced byte-identical screenshots (30-sub-r-1280.png and 30-sub-r-768.png are the same file size to the byte) — the app has no layout response below 1280. My prototypes collapse to one column around 1000px (A, C) and 1100px (B).

Tell me if mobile/tablet is in scope. If not I'll pin all three to a desktop minimum so nobody reviews a breakpoint that will never ship.

Findings on the live pages — what the rework is fixing

CUR-1The same four add-ons are sold through three different UIsHigh

Transactions, seats, smart rules and invoicing are each purchasable in more than one place, with a different interaction model each time.

Evidence: (1) Manage subscription → "Additional services" — steppers for Monthly transactions and Additional users, committed with "Update plan"; (2) same page, right column — Invoicing and Smart rules as separate cards with a tier dropdown and "Subscribe"; (3) Choose your plan — all four as steppers inside each plan card. Note that Manage subscription shows two steppers while the Essential card on Choose your plan shows four.

All three prototypes reduce this to one stepper list with one commit. Included amounts render as "included" rather than 0, so it's clear what you already have.
CUR-2No price is shown before you commit a quantity changeHigh

Both stepper surfaces change what you pay with no running total anywhere on screen.

Evidence: bumping a stepper on Manage subscription produced no change in page text beyond the number itself — no total appears near "Additional services" at any value. The only itemisation in the product is the "What's included?" popover (Product / Qty / Unit price / Amount → Essential plan 1 × $115.00, Discount −20%, Total per month $92.00) and it describes your current subscription, not the pending one.

Live total in every prototype: A in the drawer footer plus a full breakdown, B in a sticky itemised bar, C as a now→after diff in step 3 and again in the cart.
CUR-3Six names for one jobHigh

Nothing in the naming tells a user whether they're about to look, change, or pay.

Evidence: the link is "Manage subscription", the page H1 is "Subscription", it contains both a link called "Upgrade your plan" and a button called "Update plan" that do different things (one routes to plan comparison, one commits the stepper values). That link lands on a page titled "Choose your plan" at the route /organizations/billing?action=UPGRADE, which has its own "Update plan" button meaning something third.

One noun for the domain ("Billing" / "Plan & add-ons"), one verb for the commit ("Confirm changes"), and selection buttons that read as selection ("Choose Pro"), not commitment.
CUR-4Two save models on one page, neither confirms anythingMedium

Organization settings has an explicit Update for one form and silent auto-save for another, with no feedback either way.

Evidence: Organization details (name + 6 address fields) has an Update button. Notifications (activity frequency select, reconciliation reminder toggle) has none and saves on change. No toast, inline confirmation or dirty-state indicator appears for either.

One rule across all three prototypes, stated in the UI: anything that costs money confirms explicitly; preferences save instantly and say so. Every save path now produces visible feedback.
CUR-5Card and invoices both leave the product, to the same placeMedium

Two separate links open the same external portal in a new tab, and nothing warns you first.

Evidence: clicking "Change card" opened billing.stripe.com/p/session/… in a new tab; clicking "Get subscription invoices" opened billing.stripe.com/p/session/… in a new tab as well. Same hosted portal, two entry points, no external-link affordance on either.

Kept the handoff (it's Stripe's portal, not worth rebuilding) but marked both with an ↗ and copy that says where you're going and why, and grouped them so it reads as one destination.
CUR-6Broken connections are only visible if you scroll to themMedium

Two separate failures were live on the test org, both rendered mid-page, with nothing at the top saying data had stopped.

Evidence: Stripe integration showing "Connection lost" with a Reconnect button, and the accounting company showing "Disconnected" with "The accounting company has been disconnected." Both sit below the Subscription block; the page offers no summary, count or banner.

Aggregated banner at the top of all three, naming both failures and linking to each — plus a per-section marker (rail dots in A, a tab count badge in B, red card edges in C).
CUR-7A tab bar with one tab, and a second tab bar nested inside itLow

Organization settings renders a tab strip containing only "Synder sync", and the Users section inside it has its own strip ("Users" / "Accounting firm").

Evidence: role="tab" elements found on the page: Synder sync, Users, Accounting firm — one page-level tab, two nested.

A drops tabs for an anchored rail; B promotes the single tab to a real two-tab split; C replaces tabs with cards. The Users/Accounting firm split becomes two stacked sections in all three — they're not alternatives, they're both true.
CUR-8Feature comparison is collapsed on the comparison screen; the FAQ isn'tLow

The one page whose job is comparing plans hides the comparison, while seven FAQ entries sit below it — several of which exist because the UI above them doesn't explain itself.

Evidence: four collapsed accordions (Core functionalities, Add-ons, Other Synder products, Support) hold the whole feature matrix. Below them, FAQs include "What transactions am I charged for?", "Why can't I change currency when resubscribing?", "Will a discount remain if I update the plan?" and "Is tax included in the subscription price?". The transactions FAQ even instructs users to go to a different page to buy add-ons.

Comparison expanded by default in all three. The four FAQ answers that describe rules the UI should state were moved inline — what counts as a transaction sits on the transactions row, tax sits next to the total, discount persistence sits in the breakdown. The FAQ is kept in full, but it's no longer load-bearing.
CUR-9A mid-term downgrade silently forfeits what you already paidHigh

On a yearly term, downgrading in month one throws away up to eleven months of paid value, and nothing in the UI says so.

Evidence: the FAQ states "once the purchase is made, it is final and non refundable", and the update path offers no way to schedule a change for the renewal date. So a Pro customer moving to Essential pays for Essential immediately and loses the Pro balance, with no warning, no figure, and no alternative offered.

v2 itemises the loss as its own red line ("Remaining balance you would lose — not refundable"), demotes the confirm button to "Downgrade now anyway (lose $864.90)", and offers "Schedule it for 27 Jul 2027 instead" as the primary action — same lower price, nothing wasted.
CUR-10Nothing validates whether a downgrade actually fitsHigh

You can move to a plan with fewer seats than you have people, fewer smart rules than you have configured, or a transaction ceiling below your normal volume. The UI checks none of it.

Evidence: the steppers clamp to the plan's included amount and commit. No warning, no comparison against users on the account, rules configured, or transactions synced in previous months.

v2 blocks the change and names the consequence: seats ("3 people currently have access, but this leaves you 2 seats"), rules ("you have 3 configured but you'd be entitled to 0 — the extras stop running"), and volume ("your busiest month was 9,600, above the 1,000 you'd have"). Each carries a one-click fix.
CUR-11The billing page never shows what you actually useMedium

Every control sells you more capacity; nothing tells you whether you need it. The page therefore can't ever suggest paying less, which is the one recommendation a customer would trust.

Evidence: the only usage figure anywhere near billing is "Syncs left: 1000" in the top bar. No history, no peak, no comparison against the allowance.

v2 opens with six months of synced volume against the included line. On the captured demo org that reads "you're using about 42% of your allowance" — an honest prompt to consider a smaller bundle. On a busy org it flips to "you came close to your limit last month" with an upsell. Same component, opposite advice, driven by data.
SPEC-5Eight of v2's nine states are my proposal, not a captureMedium

I could only observe the Active state on the demo org. Trial, past due, cancelling, expired, Premium-quoted, no-card, loading and error are designed from first principles.

Evidence: the demo org has an active yearly Essential subscription running to 27 Jul 2027. I have no way to put it into a dunning or trial state, and none of these states are documented in Confluence or the Figma files I have.

Every number and grace period in those states is a guess — the 9-day trial, the 4 Aug retry, the 10 Aug pause. Confirm the real dunning schedule and trial length, or point me at whoever owns them, before this gets built. The shape of each state is the proposal; the timings are placeholders like the rates.

Bugs found and fixed during the build — my own, caught by verification

All three prototypes were driven with Playwright and re-tested after each fix. Listed because the first two would have produced wrong prices in a review session.

FIX-1Quantity ratchet: visiting a bigger plan permanently inflated your quantitiesFixed

Selecting a plan raised every quantity to that plan's included amount and never lowered it again. Essential → Pro → Essential left monthly transactions at 10,000 on Essential — 18 blocks "over plan" — turning a $92 subscription into $362 with no user action. Worse, a hand-set value (say 1,500) was overwritten by the Pro visit and stuck at 10,000.

Fix: quantities are now stored as user intent separately from the plan floor, and displayed as max(what you asked for, what the plan includes). Verified: round-trip returns to 1,000/$92 when untouched, and to 1,500/$104 when the user had asked for 1,500.

FIX-2C: buying historical transactions bypassed the cart it promisesFixed

Prototype C's whole premise is that nothing is charged until you confirm in one place. The Sync balance panel's "Buy" charged immediately, contradicting that on the one screen where the promise matters.

Fix: the purchase now enters the same pending-changes cart with its own now→after line and a separate one-time total. Verified the cart shows both a monthly delta and a one-time charge together.

FIX-3A: the autocharge toggle was hidden behind a Save buttonFixed

I'd wired "Autocharge me if sync balance is depleted" into the page's save bar. That's a switch that decides whether you get billed automatically — leaving it in an unsaved state is a worse failure than the inconsistency I was fixing.

Fix: autocharge saves instantly with an explicit toast stating the consequence, matching production behaviour. The save bar is now scoped to the details form only, and its copy says so.

FIX-7v2: the current price was hardcoded, so a downgrade read "+$0.00"Fixed

I stored the current monthly total as a literal 92 next to quantities that disagreed with it. Reducing seats then produced a delta of zero — the worst possible failure on a screen whose entire job is showing what a change costs.

Fix: one pricing function, priceOf(config), used for the current subscription, the pending one and the total bar. No total is ever written by hand. Verified: 1 → 2 seats now reads $92.00 → $101.60.

FIX-8v2: both downgrade guardrails were unreachable on the demo orgFixed

I built the seat and volume guardrails, then found neither could ever fire: the demo org uses 240–420 transactions against 1,000 included and holds 2 seats on a plan whose minimum is 2. Untestable code is unreviewable design.

Fix: usage and seat occupancy became scenario data rather than constants, and I added a second scenario — a Pro org, 3 people, 6,900–9,600 transactions a month. Downgrading from it trips all three guardrails, which is how the screenshots in this report were produced.

FIX-4B: the matrix caption was hidden from screen readers tooFixed

I used display:none to hide the table caption visually, which removes it from the accessibility tree — so the most complex table in the set had no accessible name at all.

Fix: visually-hidden clip technique instead, with a caption that describes the columns and that quantities are adjustable.

FIX-5C: overview cards weren't keyboard reachableFixed

The nine status cards were clickable divs — no tab stop, no Enter/Space, no focus ring. In a layout where cards are the only way in, that locks keyboard users out of every setting.

Fix: role="button", tabindex="0", an aria-label naming the destination, Enter/Space handling and a visible focus ring. Verified via the DOM.

FIX-6Premium seats rendered as "99 included"Fixed

I used 99 as an unlimited sentinel and it leaked into the UI, then read as "Unlimited included" after the first fix.

Fix: unlimited values render as "Unlimited" with no "included" suffix, in all three.

Residual issues in the prototypes — known trade-offs, not oversights

Each layout buys something and pays for it somewhere. These are the bills.

A-1A: the page is long, and billing is all of itMedium

Plan & billing alone runs roughly two viewports at 1440×900. Someone arriving to change a notification preference scrolls past every billing control to reach it. The anchored rail mitigates this but it's the only wayfinding — no collapse, no search, no persistence of which section you last used.

If A wins, consider collapsing sections by default with the rail as the opener, or remembering the last-opened section per user.
A-2A: two near-identical itemised tablesLow

The page shows a receipt for what you pay now; the drawer shows a breakdown of what you'd pay. Same columns, same styling, different meaning — currently distinguished only by a heading.

Differentiate structurally: label the drawer one as a diff (now → after) rather than a second receipt.
A-3A: the drawer covers the thing you're comparing againstLow

The 720px drawer overlays the section holding your current receipt, so the comparison you most want — what I pay now versus what I'd pay — is split across a layer boundary.

Either narrow the drawer so the receipt stays visible, or carry a compact "now: $92.00" into the drawer footer.
A-4A: rail status is colour-onlyLow

The red dots next to Integrations and Accounting carry meaning with no text or shape difference.

Add a count or an icon; the aggregated banner already carries the text, so the dots just need to not be the sole signal.
B-1B: the matrix is dense and the sticky header is tallMedium

28 rows × 4 columns, with a sticky header around 200px — roughly a fifth of a 950px viewport, held for the entire scroll. Combined with the sticky total bar at the bottom, the usable middle is noticeably compressed.

Condense the header to plan name + price once the user scrolls past the buttons, or make the feature block a separate collapsible below the quantity rows.
B-2B: you can only price the plan you've selectedMedium

Steppers appear in the selected column only, so answering "what would Pro cost at my volume" means selecting Pro first. Harmless now that FIX-1 makes selection reversible, but it's still a mode switch on a screen designed for side-by-side comparison.

Worth prototyping a variant where your quantities are applied across all three columns at once and each column shows its own total. That's the strongest version of this layout and it's a contained change.
B-3B: Cancel subscription sits in a list of utilitiesLow

The right rail is nine stacked tiles; the destructive one is last, at the same visual weight as the coupon box. It has a red heading and a gap above it, which isn't much separation for the only irreversible action on the page.

Move it out of the rail into a distinct footer zone, or behind a "More options" disclosure.
B-4B: "Choose Pro" reads like a commitLow

It's a primary-styled button that only changes selection; the actual commit is "Confirm changes" in the bottom bar. Two primary-weight buttons on screen, one of which does nothing chargeable.

Demote column buttons to outlined/selection styling and keep primary weight for the single commit.
C-1C: three save behaviours, and the rule is nearly invisibleMedium

Preferences save instantly; details, plan and balance go to the cart. That's deliberate — money confirms, preferences don't — and it's the same shape of inconsistency I flagged as CUR-4. The difference is that here it's principled, but the principle is currently stated in one panel footer.

Put the rule on the overview, next to the cart button: "Preferences save as you go. Anything you pay for waits here until you confirm."
C-2C: the read-only overview taxes the cheapest tasksMedium

Flipping the reconciliation reminder is one click today. In C it's a card click, a panel animation, then the toggle. The layout optimises for batched, expensive changes at the cost of the frequent cheap ones.

Allow the two preference toggles inline on their card — it breaks the "cards are read-only" purity but matches what people actually do most often. Worth checking against session data on how often each is touched.
C-3C: nine cards, no groupingLow

Three billing cards, five organization cards and one help card, all at equal weight in one grid. Reading order is whatever the grid does at that width.

Two labelled bands — Billing, then Organization — with help demoted to a footer link.
C-4C: "Top up balance" opens a panel called "Sync balance"Low

The card's action label and the destination's title don't match, which is a smaller version of CUR-3.

Match them. "Manage balance" on both, since the panel does more than top up.
