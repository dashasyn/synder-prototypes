Round 2 validator findings

Five validator lenses run against A, B, C and manage‑subscription‑v2: UX, Domain (accountant), Clarity (business owner), Fidelity (vs the captured production spec), and Trust (does the interface lie about money?). This page is the qualitative half of the review — the measured half, where every control was clicked in a browser, is in All 11 compared.

3 Aug 2026 · reference: demo.synderapp.com v11.7.54 captured 31 Jul 2026 · UX lens run as 3 independent instances; a finding confirmed by 2+ instances is treated as high confidence · nothing below has been applied to the prototypes

This reverses the earlier recommendation of B. The measured pass ranked B first because it is the only layout that answers "what would Pro cost at my volume" without navigating. The validator lenses put B last on three of four rankings, and two of its problems are structural rather than fixable bugs. The verdict below is the reconciled one.

Corroborated — 2+ validators, fix regardless of which layout wins

These nine are not layout opinions. They are the same defect found independently by more than one lens, and every one of them is a case of the interface stating something about money that isn't true.

X-1Coupon "Apply" is a no-op that reports success Critical

A · B · C · v2 — UX + Trust + Domain

A toast claims a discount was applied. No total, receipt or breakdown ever changes.

v2 and C make it worse by being specific: they promise the discount "appears as a line in the breakdown", and C renders "Coupon: None applied" immediately next to the success toast.

Either wire it to the total or make it an explicit "we'll validate this at checkout" state. A success toast for a change that never lands is the single most reproducible lie in all four prototypes.
X-2Historical-transaction balance never updates after purchase Critical

A · B · C · v2 — UX + Trust + Domain

The toast quotes a specific new pool size; the tile keeps showing the pre-purchase figure.

In v2, 1000 doubles as the "nothing bought yet" sentinel, so buying exactly the minimum renders "0" — the user pays and the number goes down.

One state value, rendered in both places. Same root cause as X-1: toasts are standing in for state changes.
X-3Cancelling the subscription leaves the page reading "Active" Critical

A · B · C — Trust, Critical in all three (v2 handles this correctly)

The only evidence a cancellation happened is a 4-second toast. The plan chip, the next-charge date and the cancel tile's own copy are unchanged afterwards.

v2 already shows the pattern: a cancelling state with the end date and a re-activate path. Port it into whichever shell wins.
X-4Primary price and plan display goes stale after a confirmed change Critical

B and C — Trust + Domain

B: header "Paying now", rail receipt, payment-tile amount and the matrix "Your plan" / "Upgrade" column tags are all frozen after confirm. C: the overview Plan card — plan name, $/mo, transaction allowance — is frozen after applyCart().

A is the only layout that rewrites every persistent price and plan indicator from committed state (renderPageState()), and it only does so because this exact bug was caught and fixed in the measured pass.

Whatever wins, the confirm path must re-render every element that displays a price, a plan name or an allowance — not just the one the user was looking at.
X-5v2 fabricates proration during trial Critical

v2 — UX + Trust

baseline() gives the trial state the same monthly figure as an active paid subscription, so changing plan during a trial shows a "credit for 11 unused months" and can warn the user about forfeiting money they never paid.

Trial baseline must be 0. This is the one v2 bug that would embarrass the product in front of a brand-new customer.
X-6No downgrade guardrails and no forfeiture disclosure in A, B or C High

A · B · C — Domain, High in all three (v2 is the only one that solves both)

Quantities can be reduced below live usage — seats people occupy, active smart rules, a transaction ceiling under actual volume — with no warning at all.

Worse, the copy "unused balance is credited automatically" sits on the same page as a FAQ stating purchases are final and non-refundable. Both cannot be true, and the version the user reads before deciding is the reassuring one.

v2's guardrails plus its proration disclosure ("Downgrade now anyway (lose $864.90)" against "Schedule it for 27 Jul 2027 instead") are the fix, and they need to exist in whatever ships.
X-7Autocharge toggle states no rate and no cap High

A · B · C · v2 — Clarity, High in each

The one control that can take money without the user present names no price per unit, no ceiling, and offers no user-settable limit.

Show the rate and let the user set a monthly cap. This is a production copy problem the rework inherits, not something the prototypes introduced — but none of them fixed it either.
X-8Premium steppers price things that don't count Medium

A · C — UX

Selecting Premium keeps the quantity steppers live with per-item dollar amounts, while the total resolves to "Custom" / a quote. The user configures a number that has no effect on anything.

Freeze the steppers on Premium and pass the configured quantities into the quote request instead.
X-9Abandoned draft persists and sits one click from confirm Medium

A · C — UX

Closing the drawer or panel via ✕, scrim or Escape never resets the selection to current, so the next open shows the abandoned draft — with a live confirm button next to it.

Reset on dismiss, or persist deliberately with an explicit "you have unsaved changes from earlier" banner. Silent persistence is the worst of the three options.

Layout-level — design decisions, not bugs

These are the findings that actually decide the ranking, because unlike the nine above they can't be patched without changing what the layout is.

B — the two structural problems

UX-B1The connection-failure alert lives behind the tab nobody lands on Critical

UX — confirmed by 2 of 3 independent instances (confidence 90 and 92)

The page's most urgent message — "2 connections need attention. Sales data isn't reaching your books." — is inside the Organization pane, and Billing is the pane that's open on load.

Verified in markup: the alert is at proto-b.html:316 inside <div class="pane" id="org">, while <div class="pane on" id="bill"> is the default. A user can open this screen, change plan, confirm a charge and leave without ever learning that no data is syncing.

Hoist the aggregate alert above the tab strip so it renders on both tabs, and badge the Organization tab with the issue count. This is fixable — but it means the tab split no longer owns the page's top region, which is most of B's premise.
UX-B2The matrix is the page, and it's the part both UX and Clarity understood least Critical

UX + Clarity

Steppers only exist in the currently selected plan column, so the comparison the matrix exists to enable — "which plan is cheaper at my volume" — is the one comparison it can't show. Users select, read, deselect, loop.

Also measured in the same table: 4 rows that change the bill share identical treatment with 24 static feature rows, all expanded, under a ~200px sticky header; the same row shows a stepper in one column and grey "included" text in others; "negotiated" appears where a price should be; nothing marks the column the user is on today.

Render steppers in all three columns bound to one shared quantity, and collapse the 24 feature rows behind "Compare all features". That is a different component from the one B is built around.
UX-B3Pending billing edits vanish when switching tabs High

UX

The sticky total bar is display:noned on tab change and no cue survives. Buying a seat and assigning it are on different tabs, so the one job that spans them requires a round trip through a state that silently drops work — with Reset sitting next to Confirm on the way back.

Expose the seat stepper inline in the roster, feeding the same change bar. Same fix as A's UX-A3.

C — honest about pending, dishonest about done

CLR-C1Cancel subscription is filed under a card called "Billing help" High

Clarity — reads this as deliberate

Cancellation sits alongside coupon codes and FAQs, and nothing on the nine-card overview says "subscription" or "cancel". The business-owner lens's words: it looks hidden on purpose.

This is the single worst trust signal in the set, and it's cheap to fix — but it's a symptom of C's uniform-card grid having nowhere obvious to put a destructive action.
UX-C2"Save details" doesn't save High

UX + Trust

It stages the address into the cart. The footer then resets to "No changes" and the user leaves believing the address is stored.

Non-billing edits shouldn't enter a billing cart at all. C's cart model genuinely prevents surprise charges; this is the price it pays for applying that model to everything.
UX-C1Nothing on the overview is actionable High

UX

All nine cards are read-only launchers. Nudging one transaction quantity costs card → panel → 3 wizard steps → header cart → confirm, where A and v2 do it in one interaction. The pending-changes cart also sits in the page header, far from the 620px panel where the editing happens.

Put the steppers on the plan card and reserve the panel for the plan switch itself.

A — one real gap, and it's already built elsewhere

CLR-A1"Review & confirm" never reviews Critical

Clarity

Pressing it closes the drawer and announces the change as done. No screen ever states what hits the card today, and "prorated credit" first appears after the money decision.

This is precisely v2's review step. It is the reason the recommendation below is a graft rather than a pick — A's shell is the least wrong, and its worst finding is the thing v2 already solved.
UX-A1Three commit models on one page High

UX

Instant-save toggles, a sticky save bar driven by dirty() on the details form, and the drawer's "Review & confirm" — three different rules for when a change takes effect, distinguished only by a sentence of body copy under the Notifications heading.

Give every instantly-saved control a persistent inline "✓ Saved" so the two classes are distinguishable without reading prose.
UX-A2Old total and new total are never on screen together High

UX

The 720px drawer and its scrim cover the page's "What you're paying for" table, so the current $92.00 has to be held in memory to judge the proposed figure — the comparison that makes a paid change feel safe.

Show "now → new" as two columns in the drawer footer, or render the delta (+$18.00/mo) beside the new total instead of the total alone.
UX-A3 / UX-A4Seat count is editable only in the drawer; three primary buttons compete Medium

UX

The roster that tells you whether you need a seat is a separate section from the stepper that buys one (known friction KF-2, adjacency). Separately, "Change plan & add-ons" carries the same visual weight as two Reconnect buttons, so the page's stated job has no strongest call to action.

Seat stepper into the Users section; demote in-card Reconnect to outlined and let the top-of-page error alert carry recovery.

v2 — deep scope, arithmetic problems

DOM-V1The rail breakdown doesn't sum to the total Critical

Domain (accountant)

The extra-seats line is billed at full rate while priceOf() discounts the whole bundle, so line items fail to reconcile whenever seats exceed the plan minimum.

An accountant reconciles a receipt by adding it up. If it doesn't add up, the number they trust is neither line nor total.
TRU-V1"Next charge" is hardcoded $1,104.00 Critical

Trust

renderStrip() ignores CURRENT.monthly, so after upgrading to Pro the screen reads "You pay $220" directly beside "Next charge $1,104.00 for 12 months".

Same class of bug as X-4, same fix: one source of truth per figure.
DOM-V2 / UX-V1A "Refunded" invoice row, and the comparison collapsed by default Medium

Domain · UX

Invoice history shows a Refunded row on a screen that states purchases are final and non-refundable. Separately, <details id="cmpBox"> ships closed while A and C show the plan comparison open.

Fidelity — the 43-function contract holds

  • A: 43/43 · B: 43/43 · C: 43/43 · v2: 14/14 of the Manage-subscription slice. Nothing missing, nothing dead.
  • Shared label drift vs the live capture, in all four: "Invite user" should be "Add user" (O11); "Open billing portal" / "Open portal" should be "Get subscription invoices" (S8). Invite-modal field order is Name → Email → Profession; production is Name → Profession → Email.
  • A, B and C have no loading or disabled interim state on any network-bound action (O4, O8, S13, P5) — instant toast only. v2 is the only one of the four with loading, error, trial, past-due, expired and no-card states.

Addendum — late Domain instance

A second Domain pass landed after the table above was written. Its Criticals duplicate corroborated items 4 and 6 and A's "Review & confirm never reviews". It adds three copy findings, all verified in source. None change the ranking.

  • A: "balance" means two different things on one page. proto-a.html:305 — "Anything left on your current balance is credited against the new charge automatically" — sits three lines under "Purchases are final and non-refundable" (:302), in the same column as "1,000 — Transactions left this month" (:250). Readable as unused sync allowance turning into money. Domain says Critical; I'd say High — it's corroborated item 6 wearing a vocabulary problem on top.
  • "Processing fees are free" is ambiguous — and it's in A, B and C. The validator blamed B alone and credited A. A's FAQ is correct; A's stepper description is not. proto-a.html:574, proto-b.html:514, proto-c.html:663 all read "Processing fees are free." Only v2 is right: "Processing fees are never charged" (:389). The bad reading — fees aren't synced at all — means nobody looks for Stripe/PayPal fee entries at reconciliation. One string, three files.
  • A: the sync-balance card shows remaining only. No used-of-included, no reset date, while the breakdown separately says "Monthly transactions (1,000 included)" — so 1,000 could be entitlement or nothing-consumed-yet. v2 already shows used-of-included plus a 6-month trend. Medium.

Addendum 2 — the three UX instances, reconciled, across all 11

The UX lens ran as three independent instances over the full set of eleven (A, B, C, v2, Concepts 3-in-1, V1 Unified Settings, V2 Billing Hub, V3 Org Health Dashboard, Sketch V1, Sketch V2 Tabbed, Sketch V2 Long page). A second Domain instance covered the same eleven. All three UX instances independently produced the same top four: v2 · A · C · B. v2 is now 1st on 4 of 4 completed lenses at full scope; B is last on 4 of 4.

Unanimous — 3 of 3 instances, same defect

  • A — the page teaches 3–4 different rules for "is this saved / charged yet?" Notifications commit on change, Organization details via a sticky savebar, plan & add-ons via the drawer footer, modals on their own primary button. A's own copy asserts one model that the Buy modal contradicts. (High, 85–90)
  • B — steppers are live only in the selected plan's column, and Premium never prices anything. The one question a comparison matrix exists to answer, it cannot answer; pricing another plan means selecting it, which stages a change. (High–Critical, 84–90)
  • B — 4 rows that change the bill share one uniform table with ~24 static feature rows, under ~300px of permanent chrome. The monthly "add 500 transactions" job re-scrolls a marketing comparison every visit.
  • B — seats are priced on Billing and consumed on Organization, and the bridge is a sentence, not a control.
  • C — nothing on the overview is editable; every task is a level deep. Cheapest edit = card → panel → wizard → cart → confirm, and the panel closes on every queue action. (High, 85–88)
  • C — the only commit control is a non-sticky header cart, far from the panel where the work happens; after the toast expires the pending state survives as a small badge (KF-2). (High, 84–88)
  • C — free edits and paid changes share one commit. "Nothing has been charged or saved yet" covers two different kinds of consequence in one sentence.
  • v2 — the plan switch is the most hidden control on a page called "Manage subscription." Inside a collapsed <details id="cmpBox"> behind the page's only secondary-weight button, while lower-stakes quantity steppers stay visible. Named independently by all three as the winning prototype's #1 defect.
  • v2 — guardrails are computed only in openConfirm(). The page prices an impossible configuration with a valid-looking total and an enabled CTA; the block appears only after the user commits to reviewing.

2 of 3

  • A — commit weight is inverted against reversibility. The $30 one-time historical purchase (irreversible, non-refundable) commits on one click; a $12/mo seat change (reversible, prorated) requires "Review & confirm". (High, 90)
  • A — current cost and proposed cost are never on screen together. The 720px drawer and its scrim cover the page's own breakdown table.
  • B — the "2 connections need attention" alert is inside the Organization pane and Billing is the default tab. On load the only trace is a small numeral. (Critical, 92)
  • v2 — the 8-reason cancel taxonomy is mandatory. No path for a user who has decided, and no distinction between churn and a firm routinely closing finished client orgs.

Single-instance but verified — worth fixing

  • v2 — the Smart-rules downgrade block is a genuine dead end. Confirm goes disabled with "Resolve the issue above" and the only remedy offered undoes the downgrade; no link to delete a rule. The Users block offers both "Keep N seats" and "Remove someone first". Domain corroborates from the other side: the block never names which rules stop, and smart rules drive categorisation.
  • v2 — "Schedule it for renewal instead" leaves no trace. scheduleAtRenewal() resets sel to CURRENT and reports only via toast; nothing afterwards shows a change is scheduled and there is no way to view or cancel it.
  • v2 — "See a smaller option" has no destination — a toast explaining that right-sizing is advisory and that no plan below Essential exists.
  • A — Pro Max exists only behind a Standard/Max toggle inside the Pro card and never appears in "Compare all features" (Essential/Pro/Premium). A whole price tier is unreachable from the comparison.

Round 1 is disqualified on money, not on layout

Ten of the eleven let a recurring or one-time charge commit on a single click to a success toast — no amount in the button label, no proration, no card named, no undo. Four show a total beside that button that provably excludes other priced controls on the same screen.

  • V2 Billing Hub — the five-plan tier ladder is inert and sits exactly where KF-4 says users click; the only button beside it re-selects the open tab. The plan cannot be changed anywhere on the screen. Domain adds: the same plan's entitlement reads 1,000 in the breakdown and 500 in the stepper.
  • V3 Org Health Dashboard — whole-card onclick="toggleAddon(...)": clicking a card to read it arms a +$8–$20/mo change and moves the total, and the add-ons sit below the commit button. Dirty state is inferred against a hardcoded number, so −$8 +$8 reads "no changes" while Smart Rules is off. Domain's blocker: autocharge is redefined as auto-renewal, so switching it off to avoid a renewal silently stops transactions reaching the books.
  • V1 Unified Settings — "Apply changes" commits a recurring price change on one click; calcTotal() sums only transactions and seats, so a $199/mo Invoicing tier changes the bill without changing the displayed total.
  • Concepts 3-in-1 — every action is href="#"; the commit path is unbuilt. The breakdown doesn't foot (20% of $293 is $58.60, shown as $52.00) and $241.00 is labelled both "next invoice" and "monthly total" on a yearly cycle where the invoice would be ~$2,892.
  • Sketch V1 — all seven billable rows show the identical "20% / 95 USD/mo", the table never totals, and the cancel dialog promises a free plan that does not exist.
  • Sketch V2 (both) — five add-ons share one description that only fits Invoicing, all priced $0.00 beside a live Add button, seat entitlement of 2 against a roster of 3, no total anywhere.

Effect on the verdict: it strengthens it and shifts the emphasis. v2's commit model is the part that is not negotiable — 1st on every completed lens at full scope, always for the same reason: it is the only prototype that names the amount, names the forfeit, blocks a downgrade with specific consequences, and offers a reversible alternative. A remains the right shell, but its four coexisting commit contracts have to collapse into v2's one before it ships. The cheapest confirmed fix in the set: take v2's plan switch out of the collapsed disclosure.

Verdict

Build this shell
A — one page + billing drawer

1st on Trust, 2nd on Domain, Fidelity and UX. The only layout whose confirm loop rewrites every persistent price and plan indicator it displays. Its single Critical is a missing review step — which is the exact thing v2 already built.

…with this engine
v2 — production depth

Not a competing layout, a standard: the review modal, downgrade guardrails, forfeiture disclosure and nine states. Fix its four arithmetic bugs first (X-2, X-5, DOM-V1, TRU-V1) — they're all one-source-of-truth problems.

Structural runner-up
C — overview + cart

3rd on UX across all three instances, zero Clarity Criticals, and the cart genuinely prevents surprise charges. Held back by its own honesty problems (UX-C2, X-4) and by Cancel living under "Billing help".

Why B is out, and why that's a reversal

B was my recommendation before any validator ran, on layout logic alone: it is the only shape that answers "what would Pro cost at my volume" without navigating. That advantage is real and it can be imported into A's drawer. The two problems that sink it cannot be fixed without dismantling what makes B B.

  • Last on three of four rankings, and 5 Criticals — the highest count in the set.
  • UX-B1: the "your data isn't syncing" alert is behind the non-default tab. The rework exists partly to stop burying status; B reintroduces it structurally.
  • UX-B2: the matrix is the page, and it's the component both the UX and Clarity lenses understood least. Fixing it means replacing it.
  • Worth keeping from B: shared quantities that reprice every plan column at once. Put that in A's drawer and B has no remaining advantage.

Before this becomes a spec

  • Real add-on rate card. Every price in all four files is invented. Nothing about pricing legibility can be validated until real rates land.
  • The proration / non-refundable conflict is a policy question, not a UI one. A Pro downgrade in month one can forfeit up to 11 paid months, and there is no mechanism today to schedule a change for renewal. v2 designs around it; someone has to decide whether that's the actual policy.
  • Member role. "All users see all functions" contradicts the live invite-modal copy. Either the copy is stale or a Member view is missing from all three layouts.
  • Pro Max is undocumented — it's in the plan list and nobody has said what it changes.
  • v2's timing values are guesses — trial length, the 4 Aug retry, the 10 Aug pause. 8 of its 9 states are proposal, not capture, and need a billing owner to confirm.
  • Sub-1280px doesn't exist in production. The prototypes added breakpoints; nobody has said whether that's in scope.
