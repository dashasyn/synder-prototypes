All 11 prototypes, compared

Two rounds of Manage-subscription / billing-settings prototypes measured against each other: what actually works when you click it, how much of the production functionality survives, which states exist, and whether the interface tells the truth about money.

3 Aug 2026 · reference: demo.synderapp.com v11.7.54 captured 31 Jul 2026 · 43-function inventory

Method, stated honestly. Everything in the scoreboard below is measured, not judged — every control in all 11 prototypes was clicked in a real browser, function coverage was detected from markup and rendered text, and CSS was extracted and compared against DESIGN_RULES.md. The Trust and Fidelity lenses were applied against that measured data. The three independent UX passes plus the Domain (accountant) and Clarity (business owner) lenses have since completed — their findings are in Round 2 validator findings, and they changed the verdict. Everything in the scoreboard below still stands; the "Which is better" call has been updated to the reconciled one.

Critical findings

AUTO-1V1 Unified Settings: two thirds of its controls do nothingCritical

Fifteen of twenty-two controls produced no DOM change on click, including every primary billing action.

Measured inert: Change payment method · Download invoices · Upgrade plan · Cancel subscription · Subscribe · Purchase · Get 50 free · Add integration · Reconnect · Users, plus both quantity steppers. They are styled as live buttons.

This is the prototype April's accountant persona scored highest (4/5), but as an artefact it cannot demonstrate a single billing task end to end. Any conclusion drawn from clicking it is a conclusion about a picture. Either wire the six primary actions or relabel it a static mockup so nobody reviews it as a flow.
AUTO-2Sketch V2 Long page: JS error on load, and its anchor nav is entirely deadCritical

The one thing this variant exists to demonstrate — scroll-to-section navigation — does not work.

Page error on load: Failed to execute 'scrollTo' on 'Element': the provided value is not of type 'ScrollToOptions'. All six nav items (Connections · Plan and billing · Users · Organization name · Notifications · Sync settings) are inert. This also lands squarely on known friction KF-4: step and section labels that look like navigation but aren't are the densest dead-click zone in the product.

One-line fix (scrollTo({top, behavior}) on the right element), but until it's fixed the long-page-vs-tabbed comparison cannot be made at all — you'd be comparing a working tabbed layout against a broken one.
TRU-A1A displayed the wrong subscription after a confirmed change — found and fixedFixed

Confirming a plan change in the drawer toasted success, but the page behind it kept showing the old plan and the old price. Measured: after switching Essential → Pro and confirming, the plan strip still read "Essential · $115.00 $92.00" and the receipt still itemised the Essential plan.

Exactly the failure the Trust lens exists for: the interface asserted a subscription the user no longer had, on the screen they'd check to verify what they'd just bought. Cause: the strip and receipt were static markup while the drawer computed from state.

Both now render from the same committed state as the drawer, so they cannot disagree. Verified: after confirming, strip and receipt both read Pro / $220.00.
FID-1Only one of eleven prototypes has any state other than "everything is fine"High

Ten of eleven render a single happy path. No loading, no empty, no error, no dunning, no expired, no trial.

v2 implements eight non-happy states (trial, past due, cancelling, expired, Premium-quoted, no card on file, loading skeletons, billing unavailable). A, B, C and all seven round-1 prototypes implement none. For a billing screen this is the gap that matters most: a real user meeting this page for the first time is disproportionately likely to be there because something went wrong with a payment.

Whichever layout wins should be held to v2's state list before handoff. The states are cheap to add to any of the three shells; discovering they were never designed is expensive.
FID-2No round-1 prototype covers the Update-plan page's contentHigh

The coupon field, the seven FAQs, Premium "Talk to us" and "Need a special plan?" were not detected in any of the seven.

Round 1 designed the Manage-subscription screen in isolation, so roughly a third of the inventoried functionality — everything living on /organizations/billing — has no home in it. Round 2's brief was explicitly "keep all functions" across all three pages, which is why A, B and C reach 43/43.

Not a criticism of round 1 on its own terms; it is a reason the two rounds aren't like-for-like comparable, and a reason not to hand a round-1 file to engineering as a spec.
STY-1The concepts page is built in the wrong design systemHigh

It uses Inter across more than 100 elements and a Tailwind grey palette, against a rule that names Roboto explicitly.

DESIGN_RULES.md: "Roboto (300, 400, 500, 700) — NOT Inter, NOT system-ui." Measured off-palette values: 24, including #1a1d24, #4b5563, #d1d5db. Three other round-1 files fall back to a serif default because no font-family reaches html. Fixed in round 2 during this pass: bare buttons and inputs were falling back to Arial, and tables were rendering the UA grey #808080.

Cosmetic in isolation, but it makes side-by-side comparison unfair — the round-1 concepts look like a different product, which reads as "less finished" regardless of the thinking behind them.

Which is better

Reconciled across both halves of the review. The measured pass alone ranked B first; the five validator lenses put B last on three of four rankings, and won the argument — its two worst problems are structural, not bugs. Full reasoning in Round 2 validator findings.

Build this shell
A — One page + billing drawer

1st on Trust, 2nd on Domain, Fidelity and UX. 43/43 functions, 25 controls tested, zero inert, zero JS errors — and the only layout whose confirm loop rewrites every persistent price and plan indicator it displays. Its one Critical is a missing "you'll be charged $X today" step, which is exactly what v2 already built.

…with this engine
v2 — production depth

Not a competing layout — a standard. Nine states, downgrade validation that blocks stripping seats people occupy, proration that names the non-refundable loss and offers to schedule at renewal instead. Fix its four arithmetic bugs first; they're all one-source-of-truth problems.

Dropped — was 1st on measurement alone
B — Org / Billing split

Its pricing-comparison advantage is real and portable into A's drawer. What isn't fixable: the "your sales data isn't reaching your books" alert sits behind the non-default tab, and the 28-row matrix — the whole page — is the component both the UX and Clarity lenses understood least. C is the structural runner-up instead.

Where I still disagree with the April round

April's round 2 recommended a hybrid built on the V3 Org Health Dashboard shell — which is this round's C. After the validator lenses I'm recommending A, with C as the runner-up. So the gap between the two rounds narrowed: C is now second rather than dismissed, and the shape April argued for is a live option again.

  • Choose A if the primary job is "see what I pay for and change it" in as few interactions as possible. It's the only shell that keeps every price indicator honest after a confirm, and its worst finding is a missing review step that v2 already implements.
  • Choose C / V3 if the primary job is status at a glance across many things, and if the page has to keep absorbing new add-ons — April's reasoning was that a uniform card shape scales as RevRec and others land. Its cost: every task is a level deep, and "Save details" currently doesn't save.
  • Worth noting April's per-persona scores came from persona judgement, and its top-scored prototype (V1, 4/5 from the accountant) turns out to have fifteen inert controls — so those scores were partly rating a picture.

Bottom four, and why they shouldn't be in the running

  • Sketch V2 Long page — broken anchor nav plus a JS error. Cannot demonstrate its own premise.
  • V1 Unified Settings — 15 inert controls including every primary billing action.
  • Concepts 3-in-1 — wrong font, wrong palette, lowest function coverage at 28/43.
  • Sketch V1 — nine inert nav items; useful as a record of the original sketch, not as a candidate.

The three UX passes plus the Domain, Clarity, Fidelity and Trust lenses have now completed and are written up in Round 2 validator findings — including nine defects corroborated by two or more lenses that need fixing whichever layout wins, and the six spec questions that have to be answered before any of this becomes a handoff. What remains genuinely open is pricing: every figure in all eleven prototypes is invented, so nothing about pricing legibility has been validated against real rates.
