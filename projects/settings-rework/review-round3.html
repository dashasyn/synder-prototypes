What this means for the build

Take v2 forward, with four fixes first

  1. Un-bury the plan switch. Promote the plan comparison out of the collapsed disclosure and make switching the page's primary action — 3/3 UX instances, the only 3/3 High on the winner.
  2. Fix the proration arithmetic. Decide whether the renewal date moves. If it stays, bill 11 months, not 12 — the amount due and the forfeit figure are both wrong today.
  3. Run guardrails live. Evaluate on every stepper change, not at confirm time, so the page never prices a configuration it will refuse.
  4. Separate the historical-transaction balance from the purchase quantity. They are one variable, so cancelling a purchase inflates the displayed pool.

Port in from the losers

  • From B: the sticky change bar with per-line items and a monthly delta — named the best commit affordance in the set by UX 1/3, in a prototype that finished 4th.
  • From C: the sentence “Nothing has been charged or saved yet” — Clarity called it the best sentence in any prototype.
  • From A: the line-by-line “What you're paying for” table, and its correct answers on fees, tax from the billing address, currency lock and discount portability.
  • From Round 1's Calm Overview: “Capped at $500/month — no surprise charges above this”. Autocharge in all four Round-2 prototypes takes money with no rate and no ceiling shown.

Two things only Ignat can settle

  • The credit promise. A, B and C all tell users unused balance is credited. Production terms say purchases are final and non-refundable. Either the terms are wrong or the copy is — and the answer changes three prototypes.
  • Cancellation weight. Every prototype gives cancelling the same weight as upgrading, or hides it in a card called “Billing help”. An accounting firm closing a finished client org is not churn, and no prototype distinguishes the two.
