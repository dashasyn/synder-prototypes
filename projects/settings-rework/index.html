Settings & Billing — three layouts

Organization settings, Manage subscription and Update plan, reworked three different ways. Every one of the 43 functions in the live pages survives in all three; what changes is the layout, the navigation model and where money gets confirmed.

Captured from demo.synderapp.com v11.7.54 · 31 Jul 2026 · built with the Synder React component tokens (Roboto, #0053CC)

Pricing figures are placeholders. The live steppers commit without ever showing a price, so there are no per-block rates in the UI to copy. The totals here demonstrate the pattern of live pricing, not real numbers — swap in the real rate card before anyone user-tests the amounts.

What's wrong with the three pages today

Full evidence in the validator report; the two that drove every layout decision:

  • Add-on quantities are sold in three different UIs. Steppers + "Update plan" on Manage subscription, tier dropdown + "Subscribe" on the same page, and steppers inside plan cards on Update plan. Same four products, three interaction models.
  • Nothing shows a price before you commit. Both stepper surfaces change what you pay with no running total. The only itemisation is a "What's included?" popover describing your current state, not the pending one.
  • Six names for one job: "Subscription", "Manage subscription", "Upgrade your plan", "Update plan", "Choose your plan", /organizations/billing.
  • Two save models on one page — Organization details needs an Update, Notifications saves silently, neither confirms anything happened.
  • Failures are buried. Stripe "Connection lost" and QuickBooks "Disconnected" both render mid-page with nothing at the top of the page saying data has stopped flowing.

The three layouts

A

One page, anchored sections, billing in a drawer

Everything collapses into a single Organization page with a sticky section rail. Billing stops being a destination — changing plan opens a 720px drawer over the page, with the add-on configurator and a live total.

B

Organization / Billing split, plan & add-ons as one matrix

Two top-level tabs replace the single-tab strip. Billing becomes one comparison matrix — plans as columns, quantities and features as rows, steppers in the selected column, and an itemised sticky total bar along the bottom that never leaves the screen.

C

Status overview, task panels, one pending-changes cart

The page becomes a read-only grid of nine status cards. Every change happens in a focused side panel, and anything that costs money accumulates in a pending-changes cart — one review screen, one itemised diff, one confirm for the lot.

My read

If the goal is fixing the pricing-blindness problem with the least new surface, B is the strongest: the matrix is the only layout where "what does this plan cost at my volume" is answerable without navigating anywhere, and the sticky bar makes the commit unmissable. Its risk is density.

A is the smallest change from today and the easiest to ship incrementally — you could land the aggregated status banner and the single add-on configurator without touching the rest. Its risk is page length.

C is the most opinionated and the best fit if accountants and firms batch several changes per visit. Its risk is that a read-only overview taxes the cheap tasks to help the expensive ones — worth testing against real session data before committing.
