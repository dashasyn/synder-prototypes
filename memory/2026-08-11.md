## Pricing-increase countdown banner — feedback (2026-08-11 07:58 UTC)

Ignat asked for feedback on a top-of-page banner announcing a price increase, showing a
countdown; managers want urgency but all format variants he tried "look weird — too many
symbols, no urgency feeling." Copy itself was fine, only the countdown display was in question.

**Core diagnosis given**: the format isn't the problem — the ~5-day time horizon is. A ticking
countdown over 5 days tells the reader "you've got five days," which reads as permission to wait,
not pressure to act. No display format fixes a countdown that's fundamentally not urgent yet.

**Recommended fix: escalate the display by time-to-deadline instead of picking one static
format:**
- 7+ days out: no countdown at all — just a date ("Prices increase September 1")
- 48 hours out: simple day/hour countdown ("2 days left")
- Final 24h: hours+minutes, stronger color ("4h 45m left")
- Final hour only: seconds ticking — the only point where second-level precision earns its place
Reasoning: the banner *changing* over time is what signals urgency, not the digits themselves.

**Feedback on the 4 specific variants shown:**
- `148:45:02` (colon/clock format) — weakest; reads as clock time, and forces the user to do
  mental math (148÷24) to learn it's roughly 6 days out. Never make urgency require arithmetic.
- `5d 4h 45min 4s` — confirmed Ignat's "too many symbols" instinct; 4 units shown, and unit
  abbreviations are inconsistent (single-letter d/h/s mixed with 3-letter "min").
- `5d 4h` — best of the four if something must ship immediately.
- Recommended alternative: plain "5 days left" in words, no digits-as-clock framing at all, at
  this distance from the deadline.

**Bigger point: the real urgency lever is money, not time.** "Lock in current price" is abstract
— users can't size an abstract loss. Recommended replacing/supplementing with a concrete number:
"Prices rise 20% on September 1" or "Save $240/year" — quantifies the loss instead of just the
clock, which does more persuasive work than any timer format.

**Three smaller issues flagged:**
1. The countdown "pill" element is currently the lowest-contrast piece of the banner (pale blue
   on blue) — if the deadline is the point, it shouldn't visually recede like a tag.
2. Seconds re-rendering every second creates motion next to numbers people are trying to read —
   flagged as a distraction and a potential screen-reader announcement spam issue (region should
   be muted from live-announcing every tick) if seconds are ever shown outside the final hour.
3. No dismiss control on the banner — same category of issue raised earlier for the recurring
   mapping banner (see 2026-08-07 log). Recommended: let users dismiss it during the low-urgency
   window, then have it reappear un-dismissible (or freshly) for the final 48 hours — makes its
   return meaningful rather than being ambient noise for 5 straight days.

**Status**: feedback given, no reply from Ignat yet on which direction (escalating display vs.
single static format, money-framing vs. pure countdown) he wants to take forward. Open/unanswered
as of this flush.
