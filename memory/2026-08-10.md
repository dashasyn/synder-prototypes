### 1. Stripe/Square write-access variant of the Connect card (14:13)

Ignat's context (translated): Stripe and Square need write access because of invoicing — when a
merchant sends an invoice from their accounting platform, Synder has to create the payment in the
merchant's Stripe/Square account.

**The problem:** the existing Connect card (screenshot: "Connect Shopify") promises read-only —
"No posting, editing, or deleting data" and "No invoices or records created in the platform."
For Stripe and Square that is **false**. Shipping the same card there makes the UI lie at the exact
moment it's asking for trust. Needs a separate variant, not an extra bullet.

**Proposed Stripe/Square variant:**
- Subtitle: *Let Synder import your transaction data and, if you use invoicing, collect payments for you.*
- Why Synder needs access: import transactions for the period you choose · use the data to prepare
  accurate records in your books · **record a payment in Stripe when a customer pays an invoice you
  sent from your books**
- How your data stays safe: Synder never edits or deletes your existing transactions · payments are
  recorded only for invoices you send, never on their own · your customer always confirms the payment
- Line under the card: *Only Stripe and Square need this — they're where your customers actually pay.
  Other integrations are read-only.* (Needed: after a read-only Shopify card, an unexplained
  difference reads as suspicious.)

**Also flagged on that screen:**
- "Platform 1 of 2" → **"Integration 1 of 2"** (vocabulary.md: Integration, not platform)
- A Stripe user who doesn't invoice grants write access for nothing — can the scope be requested
  conditionally, or at least should "if you use invoicing" be more prominent than the subtitle?

**⚠️ Open question blocking sign-off:** is the payment created in Stripe when the invoice is *sent*
or when the customer *pays*? My copy assumes after-payment. If Synder generates a payment
request/link at send time, the wording is wrong. **Unanswered.**

### 2. "Are you sure?" back-navigation dialog (15:26 → 15:36)

Production screenshot: user is mid-connect on the Connect integrations step, clicks Back, gets a
modal — title "Are you sure?", a yellow warning box repeating the question, buttons Cancel /
**Yes, go back** (destructive action styled as blue primary).

**Four problems:** title carries no information · question asked twice (title + end of body) ·
destructive action is the primary button · never says which or how many integrations are lost.

**Final copy (after Ignat asked for a neutral version with no platform names):**
- Title: **Going back will disconnect your integrations**
- Body: *Changing your selection resets all connections. You'll need to connect your integrations again.*
- Buttons: **Stay here** (primary) · **Go back and reconnect** (secondary)
- Alternative if weight is wanted without names: **"Going back will disconnect 3 integrations"** — count only

Rationale that stands: title carries the consequence rather than asking a question · no yellow alert
box inside a modal (a modal is already the interruption) · safe action is primary · both buttons name
their action instead of Cancel/Yes, which is genuinely ambiguous here ("cancel the going-back, or
cancel the connection?").

**Raised, not answered:** why does going back reset connections at all? If the user is only *adding*
an integration, tearing down existing connections is a technical constraint leaking into the UI. The
best version of this dialog is no dialog — preserve connections for integrations that stay selected
and warn only about ones being removed. Worth an engineering check before polishing the warning.

## Today's morning idea

**Revert-to-suggestion link on edited mapping rows** — R6 settled that amber rows in the
suggested-mapping preview mean "user edited this away from the suggestion." But nothing undoes the
edit: with a 167-option account dropdown, someone who overrides and reconsiders can't recall what
Synder proposed, and on a 50-row screen they won't remember. Fix: on any edited row, a small inline
link beside the account — "Revert to Stripe clearing (Synder)". The original suggestion is already
in memory at load, so it costs one stored value per row. Completes the amber signal (marks the change
AND offers the exit) and makes overriding feel safe enough that people review properly.
