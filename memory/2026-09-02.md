### Things worth remembering beyond this project

- **The biggest finding was a markup class, not a copy problem.** Six `<span class="toggle">`
  switches meant the entire configurator was unreachable by keyboard — with the rule off there were
  exactly three focusable elements in the dialog. The kit already ships
  `input.switch[type="checkbox"]`; using the kit's real control instead of its legacy `.toggle`
  span would have avoided the whole Critical theme. **Default to the accessible kit component.**
- **A disabled switch loses its on/off state.** The kit paints every `:disabled` switch in the grey
  off-track, so the admin-locked date row rendered *on* but looked *off* — the one fact that row
  exists to state. Dimming a control to mean "you cannot change this" must not also erase what it
  currently says. Fixed by keeping the on-track colour inside `.tog.dis` and adding the word "On".
  Caught by cropping a screenshot at 3× — invisible at page scale.
- **`aria-disabled` beats `disabled` for a blocked primary action** (keeps tab order, can explain
  itself on click) — but the kit's `.btn-disabled` sets `pointer-events:none`, which turns that into
  a dead click for mouse users. Had to restore `pointer-events` explicitly.
- **Playwright reads mid-transition colours.** A contrast check right after a class flip measured
  the button's *interpolated* background and reported a bogus 3.10:1. Wait for the transition before
  measuring. Also: `color-mix()` computes to `color(srgb 0..1)`, which a naive
  `match(/\d+/g)`-style rgb parser reads as near-black — that mis-scored the same node. Both were
  test bugs pretending to be design bugs.
- **`<details>` toggle events arrive a frame late.** The prototype stored panel-open state from the
  `toggle` event, so changing a control in the same frame as opening a panel collapsed it again.
  Read `.open` live at render time instead of trusting the event. Same failure family as the
  re-render-destroys-the-node bugs — a real user could hit it.
- **Contrast: grey_50 (`#6B778C`) fails AA** on white (4.26:1) and inside a grey chip (3.54:1) for
  text under 18.66px. That is a **design-system** problem, not a prototype one. Worked around
  locally by deriving `--color-grey-60` / `--color-green-dark` with `color-mix()` from existing
  tokens, so no raw hex entered the prototype. **The kit needs a darker grey step.** Worth raising.
- **Fidelity vs Domain can genuinely conflict.** Domain said "open invoice" must not name a set that
  includes fully paid invoices; Fidelity requires production setting names verbatim. Resolution:
  leave the two shipped setting labels untouched, fix all prototype-authored copy, and state
  explicitly that unpaid/partly paid/fully paid are all candidates. Renaming a shipped label is
  product's call, not mine.
- Where the FDD is silent on something with an accounting consequence (what document an unmatched
  payment becomes — Sales Receipt vs unapplied customer payment decides whether income is counted
  twice), **name it and flag it as an assumption** rather than leaving "processed as usual" to mean
  nothing. That is now ASSUMPTION A5.

## Afternoon — v2, rebuilt on Ignat's sketch

Ignat sent his own earlier sketch and asked me to compare it to the FDD. Then: *"This sketch looks

Answer was yes, and he said build. Live at

### What his sketch got right that mine got wrong

- **One flat list beats two numbered sections.** "Match payments to invoices where all of the
  following conditions are true:" is one readable sentence; my section 1 / section 2 split needed a
  diagram to explain itself. The scope-vs-conditions distinction is *behaviour*, not information
  architecture — it survives as: the three built-in rows are fixed and offer only the 4 pushable
  operands, added conditions offer all 10. A one-line caption between them carries the rest.
- **Naming the QuickBooks field** ("Invoice number (DocNumber)") is more honest than my
  "Compare against: Invoice number".
- **The WHEN / AND gutter** is a compact join indicator — and it turned out to be where the AND/OR
  control belongs (on the first *changeable* row, Airtable-style). That satisfies the spec's single
  combinator without a segmented control or a second heading.

### Lesson: my critique was too long and he said so

*"You gave too much text which is difficult to understand."* Twenty ranked findings when he asked a
yes/no question. The useful answer was four lines: yes, use it, here are the three things that have
to come back. **Match the answer's size to the question's size** — a "should we use this?" question
wants a verdict, not an audit. The audit can follow if asked.

### Technical things worth keeping

- **The two production stylesheets cannot share a page.** `.sds-*` legacy GSP vs the React/MUI kit
  collide on `.btn`/`.card`/`.table`. So v2 is deliberately two documents with the overlay in an
  **iframe** — which is also exactly how production works (old page hosting a React overlay), and
  the pattern Ignat pointed at with the Product mapping screenshots.
- Across an iframe boundary a focus trap is impossible; **mark the page behind `inert`** instead.
  That is what makes it a real modal. Shared state crosses by postMessage.
- **`measurePbar()` run once at load lands on the pre-webfont layout.** It measured 62px for a 40px
  bar, so a strip of the page behind showed through under the sticky bar. Fix: `ResizeObserver` +
  `document.fonts.ready`. Same latent bug existed in v1 — patched both.
- **The kit styles inputs by attribute.** `input[type="number"]` (0,1,1) beats a bare `.w-days`
  (0,1,0), so my width utility lost and the day field stretched to fill the row. Width utilities
  need two classes: `.k.w-days`. **Check specificity against the kit, not just against my own CSS.**
- **`<span>` that should be a block, twice in one day** — sample cards, then the mode labels. Title
  and description rendered as one run-on sentence. Now asserted behaviourally (innerText contains a
  newline between them) rather than by reading CSS.
- **Selects clip, they don't ellipsise.** "Memo on statement" needs ~176px; a fixed 160px silently
  truncated it to "Memo on statemen". Use `width:auto;min-width:…` for content-sized selects.
- Emoji as UI icons render as boxes in headless Chromium (and on plenty of real machines). Use
  geometric glyphs from the base font for prototype chrome.

## Late afternoon — v3, the size fix

Ignat, on v2: *"You again created a very very big screen."* Then a working HTML sketch, then a
screenshot of a third variant. Third time he has told me the screen is too big. **This is the
lesson of the day and it is not about this project:** I keep adding height by giving every fact its
own card, its own heading and its own paragraph. He keeps taking height out.

### What actually buys the space (reusable)

- **Small-caps section labels instead of cards.** `SCOPE · ALL OF THESE (AND)` costs one line; a
  card with a header, a border and 20px padding costs about 70. Same information.
- **One-line rows.** Toggle, bold name, then either inline controls or a single-sentence
  description. Two of the three scope rows need no controls at all.
- **Plain terms as a generated paragraph, not an ordered list.** Same content, a third the height.
- **Move the review aids out of the product surface.** The QuickBooks query panel and the
  sample-payment runner are not features — they are how we check the rules against the FDD's worked
  examples. Behind a presenter-bar switch, with a persistent one-line note saying they are hidden.
- **Assumption boxes default to hidden**, with a line saying how many there are. Five purple blocks
  inline were most of v2's bloat.

Result: default state ~250px of content, the whole configured rule ~1035px, in an 800px centred
sheet. v2 was a ~3000px full-page scroll.

### Copy worth stealing from his sketch

- **"Same setting as Get & Send Payments. Changing it here updates the GSP page."** Names the other
  surface *and* the direction. Mine said "one value, two places", which says neither. Also: **GSP =
  Get & Send Payments** — use the expansion in user-facing copy.
- **"Your saved rule stays on file."** R21 in five words. I had a sentence and a half.

### Judgement calls

- **Inheritance made automatic (A6).** His sketch shows it as a consequence in an info box, not a
  checkbox you must tick. Better UX, deletes a blocking error, but the FDD makes it a required
  opt-in whose absence blocks Save. Implemented his way, flagged as an assumption, told him it needs
  product. Do not silently change a spec'd blocking rule — implement, flag, name who decides.
- **Corrected the same error in three of his sketches:** the date window is **either side** of the
  payment date, not a lookback ("1 day before" → "within 90 days of" → "Look back 90 days"). When
  the same mistake recurs across drafts, say it is recurring — that is the signal it needs to go in
  the spec, not just the prototype.
- **"Engine" is our word.** It is all over the FDD and it leaked into his sketch's UI. Now asserted
  against: no user-facing string may contain it.

### Technical

- **`contentDocument` is null for a `file://` iframe** — opaque origin. Cross-frame test helpers
  must go through Playwright's `frameLocator`, not `document.getElementById('ovf').contentDocument`.
  A `waitForFunction` using it had been silently timing out behind a `.catch(() => {})`.
- **A transparent iframe** lets the host page paint the scrim while the sheet centres inside it —
  which is how a centred React dialog can live over a legacy page without sharing its stylesheet.
- Two new layout gates in the suite, both from bugs found this round: **no select may clip its
  widest option**, and **a condition row carrying a Stripe-field box must stay on one line**
  (measure the flex control row, not the grid row — the grid row includes notes and errors).
- Compacting silently dropped the **Generic customer warning (R24)**. The suite caught it. Density
  work needs a feature-coverage check, not just a look.

### The alignment diagnosis, which is the transferable bit

v3 had **four different left edges** — section label, toggle, row name, then a description column
starting mid-row — plus centred AND dividers while everything else was left. That is what "chaos"
meant. His collage has **one left edge per tier**: leading elements (headings, gutters, switches)
all start at the card padding; content after a leading control starts at one shared offset.

I now assert this: group headings and row gutters share one edge, control-row titles share a
second, rule-row content shares a third, and nothing in the rules column is centre-aligned. That
last one caught a false positive too — a `<button>` centres its own label, which is not an
alignment problem, so the check excludes buttons.

### His structural idea I would not have had

**Put the read-out in a right-hand column.** *In plain terms* stops costing vertical space and sits
beside the thing it describes. That is a better answer to "the screen is too big" than trimming
copy, which is all I had been doing.

### Switch vs checkbox

Six switches in v3. The rule: **a switch says "this is on in the product", a checkbox says "include
this in my rule".** So checkboxes on rule rows, switches only on the two outcome settings — one of
which literally mirrors a GSP toggle. Asserted by count.

### Two real bugs the suite caught in v4

- **"+ Add condition" created an instantly-invalid row.** The new row defaulted to payment metadata
  with no field name, so adding a condition immediately blocked Update. Now defaults to
  `invoice note contains memo on statement` — valid on arrival, and the common case in his sketches.
  **A control that creates an error by existing is a bug, not validation.**
- The **datalist of known Stripe field names disappeared** in the restructure. Second time a
  restructure has silently dropped a feature (Generic customer in v3). Density work needs a
  coverage check every time.

### Test-side traps

- **Unchecking a presenter-bar checkbox puts focus in the HOST document**, so `keyboard.press` never

- Asserting on `.r-note` "last" grabbed a later row's empty error slot. Scope assertions to the row

### The rule I keep getting wrong

**Enforced behaviour does not need a paragraph explaining it.** I had written:
*"Scope filters are always combined with AND — they limit which open invoices Synder looks at.
Only the four comparisons QuickBooks accepts inside a search are offered here; negations and 'is

Now: *"Which invoices QuickBooks looks at. Every row must be true."* The suite asserts the

Same for the refine group: the "AND on the first row applies to all of them" sentence is replaced

*"The user will see that and changed to or on all rows."* Words describing an interaction are worse

## Evening, part 3 — "what does 'true' mean?"

Ignat on my new sub-line *"Every row must be true"*: **"what does it mean true? Selected? If the
row is unchecked is it true? Sounds unclear."**

He was right, and chasing it found two real bugs that the copy was papering over.

1. **"True" is logician's language and it collided with the checkbox.** An unticked row is not
   *false* — it is **not in the search at all**. Copy is now *"Every ticked row has to match."*
2. **The gutter was lying.** An unticked row still printed `AND`, implying it was still part of the
   conjunction. The join word now describes the rows actually in the search: unticked shows nothing,
   the first ticked row shows `WHEN`, the rest `AND`. Untick Customer and the date row becomes WHEN.
3. **The refine-row checkbox was a dead control.** Rendered checked, bound to nothing — unticking it
   did literally nothing. Now it is real state (`c.on`): the engine skips unticked rows, validation
   stops demanding their Stripe field, the read-out counts only ticked rows, the controls grey out,
   and the join word disappears. The chevron moves to the first *ticked* row.

### The lesson

**A copy question can be a symptom.** He asked what one word meant; the honest answer was that the
word was covering for a screen that did not model the state. When copy is hard to write, check
whether the thing being described is actually coherent — three times today the copy problem was a
behaviour problem underneath.

Also: **a checkbox that changes nothing is worse than no checkbox.** Second dead-control bug in two
rounds (the other: "+ Add condition" creating an invalid row). Both were invisible to the
pointer-cursor gate because they are real `<input>`s. Worth a gate of its own — every checkbox in
the rules column must have a change handler.

## Evening, part 4 — reviewing his v2.6, then folding it in

Ignat sent a working prototype (his v2.6) and asked for a review, then: *"update the prototype
according to the file and your comments. Add errors, discard changes etc."*

### Reviewing it by running it, not reading it

Opened it in Chromium at 1440 and 1280: no JS errors, no overflow, nothing clipped, whole thing in
**803px with no scroll** — tighter than my 840. Then drove it, which is what found the seven bugs.
Reading the code alone would have missed at least three of them (the day field accepting 99999, the
silent discard, focus never entering the dialog).

**The dead-constant tell:** his `EMPTY_OPS` set was declared and never referenced. That one line
predicted the bug — "is empty" still demanded a Stripe field and still read the payment side. When
a constant exists for a rule and nothing uses it, the rule is not implemented.

### Three things of his I took

- **Nested disclosure** — the rules live *inside* the "Use custom rules" radio option. Choosing the
  mode reveals them; there is no separate card to explain.
- **Submit-time validation** — Update is always live. Press it and you get one banner at the top,
  each field marked, the message under the field, focus on the first one. Simpler than my
  aria-disabled Update plus a "Show me" jump, and it means pressing the button is how you learn
  what is missing. Kept the accessibility: banner is `role="alert"`, fields get `aria-invalid` and
  `aria-describedby`.
- His 803px density target.

### A bug both of us had

**The outcome settings were inside the custom-rules body**, so on default matching they were
unreachable — and the default matcher can also match an invoice smaller than the payment, so
"Apply as overpayment" had no defined state. They now sit outside the mode choice. I only saw it in
mine *because* I found it in his.

### The kit bites again

Moving the mode rows out of a `.card` split the control-row titles onto two left edges. Cause: **the
kit's own `.card` has 16px padding**, which had been applying to every row equally and therefore
invisibly. `.card{padding:0}` fixed it, and the alignment gate caught it — second time this week the
kit's defaults out-specified or out-padded my intent. **When a layout gate fails after a
restructure, suspect the framework's defaults before your own maths.**

### Small ones

- After a rejected Update the banner scrolled out of view, because jumping to the field used
  `scrollIntoView`. Now: scroll to top, `focus({preventScroll:true})` on the field, and "Show me" in
  the bar goes to it. Asserted that the banner is still on screen.
- Generated grammar: *"all of the 1 refine row hold"*. Singular gets its own phrasing now.
